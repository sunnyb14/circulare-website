Waste & linear systems

What does “waste” really look like in hospitality?

Waste isn’t just smelly bins out the back. It’s food you paid for but never sold, single-use items that touch a guest for seconds, and materials that could have had a second life.

  • Pre-consumer waste: over-ordering, prep off-cuts, buffet over-production.
  • Post-consumer waste: plate leftovers, bar garnishes, event scraps.
  • Packaging & materials: glass bottles, cardboard, napkins, decor.

In a linear economy, most of this follows the same path: Take → Make → Use → Dump. Materials are extracted, turned into products, used once, then sent to landfill or incineration.

Linear vs circular

From “make–use–dump” to “loop & regenerate”

Circular economy asks a different question: instead of asking “How do we dispose of this?” we ask “How do we design it so it stays useful, feeds something else, or restores nature?”

  • Design menus, storage and portions to reduce waste.
  • Turn food scraps into compost and soil instead of landfill methane.
  • Use that soil to grow plants and herbs guests can actually see.
  • Repair, reuse and repurpose decor and equipment instead of replacing by default.
Circular hospitality 101

Five ideas to shift from linear to circular in your venue.

Different thinkers use different frameworks. One simple way to look at circularity is through five verbs: Narrow, Slow, Close, Regenerate, Inform. Here’s how they show up in hospitality.

Narrow

Use fewer resources per meal served. Think smart menu design, accurate ordering, and portion sizes that match what guests actually eat.

Slow

Keep products and materials in use for longer. Repair furniture, reuse decor, choose durable items instead of disposable where it makes sense.

Close

Close the loop on materials. Turn food scraps into compost and soil. Route glass, cardboard and other streams into recycling or reuse rather than landfill.

Regenerate

Use your waste to restore rather than deplete. Use compost to grow plants, support urban greenery, or even small kitchen gardens that feed back into your menu.

Inform

Track what’s happening and tell the story. Data on waste, diversion and plants can become ESG reports, guest-facing signage, and staff motivation.

Circulare system

A plug-and-play circular system for restaurants, hotels & event venues.

Circulare is not a single product. It’s a loop. We plug into your waste, your plants and your stories, and try to handle the heavy systems work so your team can keep serving guests.

Step 01
Audit & design

Understand your venues, service style and waste flows. Map where sorting stations, collection points and plant opportunities make sense.

You: share how your operation really works. Circulare: proposes a simple, tailored loop.
Step 02
Sorting & collection

Help your team separate key streams like food, glass and cardboard without slowing service. Set up regular collection.

You: use clear signage & routines. Circulare: provides tools and picks up on schedule.
Step 03
Processing & partners

Work with vetted processors to turn food waste into compost and soil blends, and route other streams into recycling and reuse.

Circulare: handles the messy logistics and tracking behind the scenes.
Step 04
Plants & products back to you

Grow and deliver plants, soil-based products or simple items that visibly link back to your venue’s waste footprint.

You: choose where they live in your venue. Circulare: maintains and rotates.
Step 05
ESG data & stories

Provide simple numbers and storylines: waste diverted, CO₂e avoided, plants in circulation, and “this plant came from last month’s event.”

You: share with guests, teams & stakeholders. Circulare: keeps the data flowing.

Malaysia & Southeast Asia

Why Malaysia is a powerful place to prototype circular hospitality.

The region is full of exactly the kind of venues where circular systems can shine: food-obsessed cities, event-heavy calendars, and teams who already know how to make magic out of chaos.

On the ground

Think hotel buffets, wedding banquets, convention centres, cafés, mamak spots. Huge flows of food and packaging, intense peaks on event days, and a lot of potential value in what currently goes to landfill.

  • Strong food and event culture – high volumes, high visibility.
  • Growing interest in sustainability and ESG from guests & corporates.
  • Existing networks of suppliers, nurseries and processors to build with.
From pilot to network

Malaysia → Southeast Asia

The aim is to prove circular hospitality systems in one place, then grow carefully into a network that connects multiple cities and venues.

Phase 1 – Pilots
Work with a small number of committed partners in Malaysia to make the loop real.
Phase 2 – Local network
Connect more venues, refine logistics and data, share playbooks and stories.
Phase 3 – SEA circuits
Explore city-to-city expansion across Southeast Asia where hospitality waste is plentiful and stories matter.
